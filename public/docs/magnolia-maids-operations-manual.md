## 1. Introduction & Purpose

This Operations Manual is the single source of truth for how a Magnolia Maids location delivers
cleaning services. Its purpose is to:

- Guarantee a **consistent, replicable customer experience** in every home, regardless of who
  performs the work.
- Protect the brand through standardized quality, safety, and professionalism.
- Reduce liability by codifying safe chemical use, equipment handling, and client-property
  protocols.
- Provide a structured onboarding path so a new hire can reach unsupervised work quickly and
  safely.
- Give the owner and field supervisor objective tools — checklists and scorecards — to audit
  performance and coach crews.

**Manual ownership.** Cleaning standards, the approved chemical list, and the QA scorecards are
brand-wide and may not be altered locally. Owners may append local supplier contacts, insurance
details, and market-specific add-ons in the **[LOCAL]** fields.

**Distribution.** Every technician, team lead, supervisor, and owner must acknowledge receipt and
completed training on the Training Sign-Off Log (Appendix D) before performing unsupervised work.

### 4.9 Airbnb turnover checklist (Lake Texoma standard)

A turnover is not a light clean — it is a **reset to a photographed standard** under a hard
deadline. The crew works the full Standard checklist for the property plus everything below.

**Arrival**
- Confirm checkout has occurred and note the departure time.
- Walk the unit with the phone camera before touching anything; photograph any damage, stains,
  missing items, or excessive mess. This is the damage record and must be filed the same day.
- Bag and remove all guest trash, food, and left-behind items; left-behind valuables are bagged,
  labeled with the unit and date, and returned to the host.

**Linens and beds**
- Strip all used linens and towels; count them out and count replacements in.
- Inspect every sheet, pillowcase, duvet cover, and towel under light for stains, hair, and tears.
  Anything questionable is pulled from service, not re-hung.
- Make every bed to the staging photo: mattress protector, fitted sheet, flat sheet, duvet, pillow
  count and arrangement, throw placement.
- Check under beds and behind headboards for guest items.

**Kitchen reset**
- Empty and wipe the refrigerator, discarding all guest food unless the host directs otherwise.
- Run and empty the dishwasher; every dish, glass, and utensil is put away clean and dry.
- Verify inventory against the host's kitchen list; note shortages in the report.
- Reset coffee station, trash liners, and dish soap.

**Bathrooms**
- Full bathroom procedure, then stage: folded towel set per the photo, fresh soap and paper,
  toilet paper spare visible, hair dryer coiled and stored.

**Restock (host-supplied consumables)**
- Toilet paper, paper towels, hand soap, dish soap, dishwasher pods, laundry detergent, trash
  bags, coffee, filters, sugar, creamer, water if provided.
- Anything at or below par level is listed in the turnover report the same day.

**Staging and final**
- Match the listing photos room by room: pillow arrangement, remote placement, chair angles,
  blinds position, lights and thermostat setting per host instruction.
- Check batteries in remotes and smoke detectors when the host requests it.
- Confirm the welcome materials, lockbox code card, and house rules are present and clean.
- Photograph every finished room and submit the turnover report: photos, damage notes, restock
  needs, and any maintenance issue (leaks, burnt bulbs, AC problems).

**Turnover report — required fields:** property, date, crew, checkout condition rating (1–5),
damage found (Y/N + photos), items left behind, linens pulled from service, restock needed,
maintenance flags, time in / time out.

### 4.10 Post-event clean checklist

Booked as Post-Event Clean; typically same-day or next-morning after a gathering.

- Full trash-out first: bag and remove all party waste, bottles, cans, and food to the client's
  bins or, when contracted, off site.
- Collect and consolidate dishware, glassware, and serving items; run dishwasher loads and hand-wash
  what cannot be machine washed.
- Spot-treat carpet and upholstery spills immediately (blot, never scrub; no wet extraction — flag
  anything beyond spot treatment).
- Sticky-surface pass: counters, tables, bar tops, cabinet fronts, refrigerator handles, light
  switches, and door handles.
- Remove decorations, tape, and confetti only when the client authorizes it in the booking notes.
- Bathrooms receive the full bathroom procedure with extra restock (paper, soap, towels).
- Floors last: sweep, vacuum edges and corners for crumbs and glitter, then mop.
- Final: outdoor entry and patio area tidied if in scope, all removed waste accounted for, home
  returned to its normal furniture layout.

### 4.11 Apartment and property-management turn checklist

Booked through the apartment turnover service line; the client is a property manager, not a
resident, and the unit must be **lease-ready**.

- Confirm unit number, access method, and turn deadline with the property office before starting.
- **Trash-out:** remove everything the previous resident left, including closets, patio, and
  storage; document anything too large for the crew and notify the office immediately.
- Full Move-In/Move-Out checklist for every room, including insides of all cabinets, drawers,
  closets, and appliances.
- Wipe all baseboards, door frames, switch plates, and outlet covers.
- Clean all window tracks, sills, blinds, and interior glass.
- Clean HVAC vent covers; note filter condition (crews do not replace filters unless contracted).
- Wipe out the water heater and utility closets; sweep balconies and patios.
- Spot-treat wall marks; photograph any damage or paint needs rather than attempting repair.
- **Unit-ready photo set:** every room, both bathrooms angles, inside the refrigerator and oven,
  and the entry. Photos are attached to the turn report for the property manager.
- **Key handoff:** keys, fobs, and access cards returned to the office and logged by name, unit,
  date, and time. Never leave keys inside the unit or in the lockbox without written instruction.
- Turn report: unit, crew, time in/out, trash-out volume, damage found, maintenance flags,
  photo set attached.

## 5. Room-by-Room Cleaning Procedures

Each procedure follows the top-to-bottom, back-to-front method and references approved chemical
codes (Section 6) and tools (Section 7). The sequence matters — it prevents recontaminating
surfaces already cleaned.

### 5.1 Kitchen procedure

1. Clear counters of small items; place dishes in the sink or dishwasher per client preference.
2. Dust light fixtures, cabinet tops, and vents (extendable duster, dry).
3. Wipe upper cabinet exteriors and the microwave interior (C-2, yellow microfiber).
4. Clean stovetop, knobs, and backsplash (C-2; finish glass cooktops with C-6 after degreasing).
5. Wipe appliance exteriors, following the grain on stainless steel (C-5).
6. Clean and disinfect countertops (C-1, yellow microfiber) and observe the label dwell time.
7. Clean and shine the sink and faucet, removing water spots (C-2, then C-5 for shine).
8. Wipe lower cabinet fronts and handles.
9. Sweep, then mop with C-3 (flat mop, green pad).
10. Empty trash, reline, and wipe the bin rim and exterior.
11. Final check: no streaks on stainless, no standing water, no crumbs at floor edges.

### 5.2 Bathroom procedure

1. Apply C-4 to the toilet bowl, exterior, and base; allow the full label dwell time (5 minutes
   minimum) while other work proceeds.
2. Dust light fixtures, exhaust fan cover, and mirror frame.
3. Spray and scrub tub, shower walls, tile, and glass with C-4. For hard water and soap scum, use
   C-7 on fixtures and glass — **never** applied to the same surface as C-4 without a full rinse in
   between (see 6.4).
4. Rinse and squeegee glass and tile; dry with red microfiber to prevent spotting.
5. Clean and disinfect the sink, faucet, and counter with C-4 (red microfiber — bathroom only).
6. Return to the toilet: scrub the bowl, wipe the seat, lid, base, and behind the hinges, then the
   tank and flush handle last.
7. Clean the mirror streak-free with C-6 and a blue microfiber.
8. Wipe cabinet exteriors.
9. Empty trash and reline.
10. Sweep and mop the floor last, working backward out of the room (C-3, or C-4 where disinfection
    is needed).
11. Final check: no hair in drains or corners, no soap film, streak-free glass, grout free of
    visible mildew.

> **Cross-contamination rule.** Red microfiber and toilet brushes are bathroom-only and never enter
> a kitchen. Color coding is mandatory brand-wide (Section 7.3).

### 5.3 Bedroom procedure

1. Open blinds and curtains; dust window treatments.
2. Dust ceiling fan blades and light fixtures top-down.
3. Make the bed: straighten and tuck sheets, fluff pillows, fold throws.
4. Dust all furniture with a damp yellow microfiber; use C-8 on finished wood only.
5. Dry-dust electronics — never spray chemical toward a screen or vent.
6. Empty and reline trash bins.
7. Neaten visible clutter; place stray items together on a visible surface, never hidden away.
8. Vacuum edge-to-edge including accessible under-bed area; sweep and mop hard floors with C-3.
9. Final check: bed made to standard, flat surfaces dust-free, floor clean to the edges.

### 5.4 Living and common area procedure

1. Dust ceiling fixtures, fan blades, and upper shelving.
2. Dust and wipe furniture, entertainment centers, and decor (C-8 for wood, dry cloth for
   electronics).
3. Fluff cushions and pillows; fold blankets.
4. Surface-vacuum upholstery with the correct attachment — no wet cleaning.
5. Clean glass tabletops with C-6 on the cloth; screens get a dry or barely damp microfiber only.
6. Wipe baseboards on Bright and Deep cleans.
7. Vacuum fully, then mop hard flooring with C-3.
8. Final check: cushions arranged as the client keeps them, no dust visible under direct light,
   corners free of debris.

### 5.5 Home office procedure

1. Dust light fixtures and shelving.
2. Dust around — never move — papers, files, and documents without explicit permission.
3. Wipe non-electronic surfaces with C-1.
4. Dry-dust monitors, keyboards, and printers; no liquid near electronics.
5. Empty trash and recycling.
6. Vacuum and mop.
7. Final check: papers undisturbed, cables as found, surfaces dust-free.

### 5.6 Move-in / move-out additional steps

1. Confirm the property is vacant, or note occupied areas to skip, before starting.
2. Complete every **M** item in Section 4 for every room.
3. Clean the interior of every cabinet, drawer, and closet — vacuum shelving before wiping.
4. Clean the interior of all major appliances.
5. Clean all window tracks, sills, and interior glass.
6. Wipe every baseboard, door frame, and switch plate in the property.
7. Spot-treat wall marks with C-9, tested in an inconspicuous area first. Do not use on flat or
   freshly painted walls without written approval.
8. Final walkthrough with photo documentation of every room for the client, landlord, or property
   manager record.

### 5.7 Airbnb turnover procedure (time-boxed)

Turnovers run to a clock. The target sequence for a 2bd/2ba lake unit is 90–120 minutes with two
technicians:

1. **0–10 min — Document and strip.** Photo walk, trash-out, strip all linens and towels into the
   laundry bag, start the first wash if the unit has a laundry.
2. **10–25 min — Kitchen reset.** Refrigerator emptied and wiped, dishwasher started or emptied,
   counters and appliances cleaned, inventory checked.
3. **25–55 min — Bathrooms.** Full Section 5.2 procedure in each bathroom, then staging.
4. **55–80 min — Bedrooms and living.** Beds made to the staging photo, dusting, glass, surfaces.
5. **80–100 min — Floors and staging.** Vacuum and mop through the unit ending at the door; final
   staging against the listing photos.
6. **100–120 min — Report.** Photo set, damage notes, restock list, maintenance flags submitted
   before leaving the property.

If a turnover cannot be completed to standard before the next check-in, the Team Lead calls the
supervisor immediately. The supervisor — never the crew — communicates with the host.

### 6.2 Prohibited and restricted substances

- **No mixing.** Bleach with ammonia or with any acid produces toxic gas. Chemicals are never
  combined outside manufacturer-labeled use.
- **No unlabeled or repackaged chemicals** in the field. Every bottle carries a secondary label.
- **No client-supplied chemicals** without a signed Client Chemical Waiver (Appendix E), a
  compatibility review, and an available SDS.
- **No abrasive powders or steel wool** on stainless, glass cooktops, natural stone, or fiberglass.

### 6.3 Green track [LOCAL]

Households with allergies, infants, or chemical sensitivity are offered the green line: C-1G
through C-9G equivalents mirroring the categories above, using EPA Safer Choice or Green Seal
certified products. Green-line accounts are flagged in the customer's home profile (Section 8) and
the correct kit is confirmed loaded before departure.

### 6.4 Compatibility and storage chart

| C-1 / C-4 disinfectants | Acid descalers | Eye level for easy label checking |
| All chemicals | — | Original caps and triggers on assigned bottles; never transfer between unlabeled containers |

## 8. Technology: The Magnolia Maids Customer Pipeline

The software platform is a brand standard, not an optional tool. It is how the company holds a
consistent customer experience without a call center. Every owner and supervisor is expected to be
fluent in it.

### 8.1 How a customer becomes a job

```text
   Ad / search / referral / Facebook / Nextdoor
                    |
        Instant quote on the website  -----> price range shown immediately
                    |
      Booking request  or  quote request (office follow-up)
                    |
     Confirmation email + internal alert to the office
                    |
       Admin dashboard: assign, schedule, confirm
                    |
     Reminder email before the visit --> crew performs the job
                    |
     Customer account portal: history, rebook, change requests
```

### 8.2 The quote engine

Pricing lives in one place and every surface reads from it — the website pricing page, the booking
wizard, the instant-quote page, the chat and voice assistant, and the emailed quote. **Prices are
never quoted from memory.** If a crew, an owner, or an assistant produces a number that disagrees
with the quote engine, the quote engine is right and the discrepancy is reported the same day.

A quote is built from: service line, bedrooms, bathrooms (half baths at half), frequency discount,
and the +15% (minimum $25) upper band for condition.

### 8.3 Intake surfaces

| Surface | Purpose | Where it lands |
|---|---|---|
| Instant quote (homepage and pricing page) | Self-serve price in seconds | Becomes a booking or a lead |
| Booking wizard | Full booking with address, date, and time-slot availability rules | Bookings |
| Instant quote page (`/quote`) | Distraction-free single-purpose form used in ad and email follow-up | Leads |
| Campaign landing pages | Seasonal offers | Leads, tagged by campaign |
| Apartment / property inquiry form | B2B turn work | Property inquiries |
| Careers form | Hiring | Applications |
| Franchise interest form | Ownership candidates | Franchise inquiries |
| Chat and voice assistant (Maggie) | Answers questions, quotes from the same engine, hands off to a form | Leads and transcripts |

**Returning contacts.** If an email or phone number already exists, the new submission is appended
to the existing record and logged as activity rather than creating a duplicate. The dashboard
raises a "returning contacts" alert so the office sees that an existing customer updated details.

### 8.4 Attribution

Every submission stores UTM source, medium, campaign, referrer, and landing page. Marketing spend
decisions are made from that data, not from impressions. Review source mix monthly.

### 8.5 Automated email program

| Email | Trigger | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Booking confirmation | Booking submitted | Immediate |
| Quote request received | Quote form submitted | Immediate |
| Internal alert | Any submission | Immediate to the office |
| Visit reminder | Upcoming scheduled clean | 1 day before |
| Lead follow-up (instant quote invite) | New advertising lead | ~3 minutes after the lead |
| Hiring follow-up | New job applicant | 1–2 hours after the application |
| Campaign thank-you | Campaign lead | Immediate |
| Franchise candidate confirmation | Franchise interest submitted | Immediate |

Templates are editable by the owner in the admin email center, including subject, hero copy, body,
and footer. Two rules: every template must read correctly when optional fields are blank, and no
template makes a claim the company cannot honor.

### 8.6 The customer account portal

Customers can create an account to see upcoming appointments, service history, and to rebook. The
portal is also where the **home profile** lives, and the home profile is what the crew relies on:

- Entry method, entry code, lockbox location, alarm instructions, parking notes
- Rooms to focus on and rooms to skip
- Product and scent preferences, including the green track
- Supplies notes and standing add-ons
- Pets: name, kind, temperament, and where they will be during the clean
- Preferred frequency, day, and arrival window

**Rule:** the home profile is read before every visit and updated after any visit where reality
differed from the profile. A change requested verbally on site is entered into the system the same
day — verbal-only changes do not exist.

### 8.7 Change requests

Customers submit reschedule, skip, cancel, or scope-change requests through the portal. The office
responds inside the system so there is one record. Target response: same business day.

### 8.9 Data handling

Client addresses, entry codes, alarm instructions, and photos are confidential business records.

libraries, and never discussed outside the crew. Job photos are taken in the service app or company
account and deleted from any personal device immediately after upload. Violation is grounds for
termination.

## 9. Team Training Modules & Certification Tracking

All new hires complete Modules 1–7 before working unsupervised. Supervisors re-certify staff
annually and after any incident or QA score below 80.

### Module 1 — Orientation & professionalism
- Brand story, service promise, and the locally-owned standard
- Uniform, grooming, punctuality
- Client communication: what to say, what to escalate, how to handle out-of-scope requests
- Confidentiality: no photos or social posts of client homes, no discussing other clients
- **Assessment:** written quiz (90% to pass) plus a client-greeting role play

### Module 2 — Core technique & standards
- Top-to-bottom, back-to-front method
- Section 4 checklists and Section 5 procedures
- Time targets per room by home size
- Tool technique: microfiber folding, mop handling, vacuum patterns
- **Assessment:** supervised practice clean scored against the Section 10.3 self-inspection

### Module 3 — Chemical safety & equipment
- Reading SDS sheets; secondary container labeling
- The approved list, dilutions, dwell times, and compatibility
- Never-mix rules; spill and exposure response
- PPE and ventilation
- **Assessment:** written exam on compatibility — **100% required**, zero tolerance — plus a PPE
  demonstration

### Module 4 — Safety & emergencies
- Lifting, step-stool safety, repetitive strain prevention
- Wet-floor and slip hazard control
- Client medical emergency, fire, or break-in discovery
- Bloodborne pathogen and biohazard non-scope boundaries
- Incident report walkthrough (Appendix G)
- **Assessment:** scenario-based verbal assessment with supervisor sign-off

### Module 5 — Customer service & problem resolution
- On-site de-escalation and when to call the supervisor
- Scope boundaries (Section 2.4)
- Documenting pre-existing damage before starting
- Before/after photo standards
- **Assessment:** dissatisfied-client role play

### Module 6 — Quality assurance
- The scorecards from the auditor's point of view
- Building the pre-departure self-inspection habit
- How QA scores affect reviews, incentives, and route assignment
- **Assessment:** mock self-audit reviewed by a supervisor

### Module 7 — Specialty lines: turnovers, events, and apartment turns
- Airbnb turnover: the clock, staging to listing photos, linen inspection, restock par levels,
  damage documentation, and the turnover report
- Post-event: trash-out sequencing, spot treatment limits, glassware handling
- Apartment turns: lease-ready standard, unit-ready photo set, key handoff and logging
- Property-manager communication: report, never negotiate
- **Assessment:** one supervised turnover and one supervised turn, each scored 90+

## 12. Durant & Lake Texoma Market Playbook

This chapter is local context. A location in another market replaces it with its own.

- **Core service area:** Durant and Bryan County, extending to Lake Texoma, Kingston, Cartwright,
  Calera, Caddo, Colbert, and Mead. Confirm drive time before accepting work at the edges — a job
  that cannot be reached on time is a job that damages the brand.
- **Seasonality:** short-term rental turnovers peak from spring through early fall around the lake,
  with weekend check-in and check-out clustering that makes Friday and Sunday the highest-pressure
  days. Holiday and event work concentrates in November and December, and graduation and family
  gathering work in May.
- **Weekend staffing rule:** turnover-capable crews are scheduled first for Friday and Sunday
  windows; recurring residential work is scheduled Monday through Thursday wherever the customer
  will allow it.
- **Referral culture:** in a market this size, the neighbor's opinion is the marketing channel.
  Every complaint is treated as a public event, and every recovery is treated as an investment.
- **Local partners to develop:** short-term rental hosts and property managers around the lake,
  apartment communities and student housing near Southeastern Oklahoma State University, realtors
  handling move-out cleans, and event venues.
- **Weather:** heat and storm season affect both scheduling and drive time. Crews confirm access
  and road conditions before lake-area routes and never take on standing-water cleanup.
